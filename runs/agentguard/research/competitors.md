# Competitors — teardown and positioning

**What this is**: a named-competitor teardown table covering commercial vendors, open-source tools, first-party platform features, and the "do nothing" status quo, followed by a two-axis positioning read of where the white space actually sits.

**Why it exists**: BRIEF.md's Competition section (as written before this research) named only the four academic benchmarks and explicitly deferred commercial competitors to this phase. That deferral is now resolved — and the answer is more crowded than the brief assumed. The failure this document prevents: a positioning deck that claims "no one does this" against a category that raised $3.6B and consolidated five vendors into incumbents in the last 18 months.

**How to read it**: the table is ordered by how directly each entry threatens the specific wedge, not by size or funding. A skeptic should start with Adversa AI (confirmed direct commercial competitor, closest claimed mechanism match) and AutoDojo (closest academic prior art, adopted rather than out-competed) — if AgentGuard can't articulate a clear, distinct answer to both, the positioning isn't done. NIST CAISI's result is evidence for the thesis, not a competing product — see its row for why.

**Depends on / feeds**: built from `research/sources.md` and `research/landscape.md` §1. Feeds `strategy/positioning.md` and `strategy/market_sizing.md` directly — this document does not compute TAM/SAM/SOM itself.

## Positioning read — the two axes that matter

**Axis 1: Threat-model rigor — static/fixed attack set ↔ live adaptive, feedback-only evolution.**
Most of the table sits closer to "static" than marketing suggests: Garak, InjecAgent and Agent Security Bench (genuinely fixed-attack-set benchmarks by design), and most guardrail/classifier vendors test against a fixed probe or attack library. AgentDojo itself is not on the "static" end — it is an extensible environment; what's fixed is only the attack distribution it originally shipped with. A smaller cluster (Adversa AI, AutoDojo, NIST CAISI's exercise, Async Control, MUZZLE) demonstrates or claims genuine round-over-round adaptation.

**Axis 2: Delivery form — one-off academic/research artifact ↔ standalone, continuously available commercial service.**
AutoDojo, MUZZLE, PI-Hunter, and the NIST CAISI exercise sit at the "one-off artifact" end (a paper, a harness, or a single human exercise — not a maintained, repeatable service). Microsoft's Red Teaming Agent and Cisco's AI Defense sit at "bundled platform feature." Adversa AI, Mindgard, Straiker, HiddenLayer, SplxAI, Repello, and Giskard sit at the "standalone commercial service" end.

**Where the white space actually sits — resolved (see BRIEF.md/ASSUMPTIONS.md A9)**: neither axis alone locates it. Adversa AI already occupies "adaptive AND standalone commercial service." AutoDojo already occupies "adaptive AND rigorous, in the AgentDojo environment." What nothing in this table occupies is a **third axis**: a standing, reproducible, budget-matched comparison **across multiple defense families on the same target**, with attack success and legitimate-task utility reported together. AutoDojo stays within one framework and does not measure utility. NIST's exercise tested one model with human red-teamers, once. Adversa AI's engagements are proprietary and per-customer, producing no publishable comparative science. AgentGuard's differentiation is this comparison protocol, not the underlying adaptive-attack technique — which is why AutoDojo is adopted as one attacker configuration inside AgentGuard's design rather than treated as a rival to out-innovate on technique.

## Recommended next 3

1. **Build `strategy/positioning.md` around the comparison protocol** (budget-matched, utility-paired, cross-defense-family, reproducible) — not around out-adapting Adversa AI or out-researching AutoDojo, neither of which is a fight AgentGuard needs to pick on attack technique alone.
2. **Name Adversa AI as a direct competitor and AutoDojo/NIST CAISI as prior art/evidence, explicitly distinguished, in the pitch deck and VC memo's competition slide** — collapsing all three into one undifferentiated "competitors" bucket would misrepresent the research.
3. **Treat the four academic baseline benchmarks (AgentDojo, InjecAgent, ASB, BIPIA) as ground truth/data sources and one attack technique (AutoDojo's) as an adopted component, and treat Adversa AI, Mindgard, Straiker, HiddenLayer, and Microsoft's Red Teaming Agent as the real business-level competitive set** in every downstream artifact.
