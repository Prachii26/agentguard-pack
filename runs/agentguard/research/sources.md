## Baseline benchmarks (the four named in BRIEF.md)

- **[S1]** "AgentDojo: A Dynamic Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352 — accessed 2026-09-09 — publication date (v1 2024-06-19, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks Track), authors/institution (ETH Zurich/Invariant Labs), utility and ASR metric definitions. **Correction**: AgentDojo's own abstract describes it as "a dynamic environment," an extensible framework built for evaluating adaptive attacks and defenses — not a static test suite. What is fixed is the *shipped attack distribution* used in the original paper's evaluation, not the framework itself. Explicit statement that adaptive-attack evaluation using this framework was left as future work at time of publication.
- **[S2]** AgentDojo NeurIPS 2024 poster page — https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/97522 — accessed 2026-09-09 — confirms NeurIPS 2024 venue.
- **[S3]** InjecAgent: "Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injections in Tool-Integrated Large Language Model Agents" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02691 — accessed 2026-09-09 — publication date (v1 2024-03-05), ACL 2024 Findings venue, static fixed-attack-set design (1,054 test cases, 17 user tools, 62 attacker tools, one fixed "enhanced setting" prefix).
- **[S4]** InjecAgent, ACL Anthology record — https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.624/ — accessed 2026-09-09 — venue confirmation.
- **[S5]** Agent Security Bench (ASB): "Formalizing and Benchmarking Attacks and Defenses in LLM-based Agents" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02644 — accessed 2026-09-09 — publication date (v1 2024-10-03), authors/institutions (Zhejiang University, Rutgers), scope (10 scenarios, 10 agents, 400+ tools, 27 attack/defense methods, 7 metrics, 84.30% highest reported ASR), static/predetermined attack design.
- **[S6]** BIPIA: "Benchmarking and Defending Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on Large Language Models" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14197 — accessed 2026-09-09 — publication date (**v1 2023-12-21** — confirms BIPIA predates the other three), Microsoft Research/USTC authorship, "first benchmark for indirect prompt injection" claim, static attacks with both black-box and white-box *defense* settings.
- **[S7]** BIPIA, Microsoft Research publication page — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/benchmarking-and-defending-against-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks-on-large-language-models/ — accessed 2026-09-09 — institutional confirmation.
- **[S8]** BIPIA, KDD '25 ACM Digital Library record — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3690624.3709179 — accessed 2026-09-09 — confirms KDD 2025 venue (separate from arXiv preprint date).

**Correction record**: prior belief in ASSUMPTIONS.md A1 that "all four benchmarks published 2024" is **confirmed wrong for BIPIA** (Dec 2023, not 2024) and now dated precisely for the other three: AgentDojo June 2024, InjecAgent March 2024, ASB October 2024.

**Second correction record (2026-09-09, founder spot-check)**: AgentDojo is not a static benchmark/framework — it is an extensible environment explicitly designed to host adaptive attacks and defenses (S1). What is fixed is the *attack distribution AgentDojo shipped with in its original paper*, which is the precise thing AutoDojo's own authors mean when they call it "inherently static, generating a fixed distribution of IPI attacks" (S13). Every downstream reference to "AgentDojo is static" must say "AgentDojo's shipped attack distribution is fixed," never "the AgentDojo framework is static." InjecAgent and Agent Security Bench, by contrast, genuinely are fixed-attack-set benchmarks by design (no extensibility claim in their own abstracts) — the correction applies to AgentDojo specifically, not to all four benchmarks uniformly.

## Adaptive-attack academic prior art — direct bearing on the differentiation claim

- **[S9]** "The Attacker Moves Second: Stronger Adaptive Attacks Bypass Defenses Against LLM Jailbreaks and Prompt Injections" — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09023 — accessed 2026-09-09; posted 2025-10-10 — 14 authors across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind (incl. Nicholas Carlini, Florian Tramèr); adaptive attacks broke 12 published defenses, most to >90% ASR, that had reported near-zero vulnerability under static evaluation; 500-participant human red-team competition reached 100% success against all tested defenses. **Single strongest academic validation of AgentGuard's core thesis (adaptive >> static), but also proof the thesis is already published, not proprietary.**
- **[S10]** Simon Willison, "New prompt injection papers" (roundup incl. Attacker Moves Second, MUZZLE, Meta's Agents Rule of Two) — https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/2/new-prompt-injection-papers/ — accessed 2026-09-09 — secondary summary corroborating S9 and introducing Meta's "Agents Rule of Two" defense-taxonomy framing (2025-10-31).
- **[S11]** "Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on LLM Agents" — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.00061 — accessed 2026-09-09; posted ~2025-03 — tests three defense categories (detection-based, sanitization, reasoning-modification) against LLM *agents specifically*; per-defense adaptive attack designed for each; adaptive succeeds where the same defenses appeared strong against static attacks.
- **[S12]** MUZZLE: "Adaptive Agentic Red-Teaming of Web Agents Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks" — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09222 — accessed 2026-09-09; dated 2026-06 (v2) — Mozilla + Northeastern University authors; fully automated framework that iteratively refines injection tactics based on observed defense behavior against browser-agent defenses (same-origin policy, site isolation, injection detectors); open research framework, scoped to web/browser agents, not general tool-use.
- **[S13]** AutoDojo: "Adaptive Black-Box Attacks Reveal the Limits of IPI Defenses and Task-Specification Effects in LLM Agents" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15057 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2026-06-19 — Washington University in St. Louis, Texas A&M, Johns Hopkins; **closest single direct match to AgentGuard's stated mechanism**: black-box LLM-driven iterative attack optimization against a specific deployed defense, built inside the AgentDojo *environment* (not a fixed competitor to it) to go beyond AgentDojo's originally-shipped fixed attack distribution — the paper's own framing: "commonly used benchmarks for evaluating defense, such as AgentDojo, are inherently static, generating a fixed distribution of IPI attacks." Recovers 28% ASR overall (64% on "action-open" tasks) against a filter defense that drove the shipped-distribution's static ASR to 0%; public GitHub, positioned as a reusable evaluation configuration one can run on top of AgentDojo, not a one-off study, and does not itself measure utility.
- **[S14]** AutoDojo GitHub repository — https://github.com/xhOwenMa/AutoDojo — accessed 2026-09-09 — confirms open-source/academic (not commercial) release status.
- **[S15]** PI-Hunter: "Automated Red-Teaming for Exposing and Localizing Prompt Injections" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12737 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2026-06-10 — agentic auditing framework that iteratively evolves test cases through feedback-driven exploration to surface latent injection vulnerabilities.
- **[S16]** PIArena: "A Platform for Prompt Injection Evaluation" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08499 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2026-04-09, accepted ACL 2026 — unified benchmark/attack/defense/evaluator platform including "a dynamic strategy-based attack that adaptively optimizes injected prompts based on defense feedback"; domain coverage (QA/RAG/code-gen confirmed; agentic tool-call coverage unconfirmed from abstract).
- **[S17]** PIArena GitHub repository — https://github.com/sleeepeer/PIArena — accessed 2026-09-09.
- **[S18]** PISmith: "Reinforcement Learning-based Red Teaming for Prompt Injection Defenses" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13026 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2026-03-13 — RL-trained (GRPO-family) attacker in a black-box query-only setting; notes naive RL fails against strong defenses due to reward sparsity, requiring specialized reward shaping.
- **[S19]** "Learning to Attack and Defend: Adaptive Red Teaming of Language Models via GRPO" (AdvGRPO) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09701 — accessed 2026-09-09; published 2026-06-08 — Microsoft authors; co-trained attacker/defender via RL with a curriculum from single-turn to closed-loop multi-turn attacks; general chatbot/jailbreak scope (HarmBench), not agent tool-use IPI specifically.
- **[S20]** RedEvoAgent: "Automatic Red-Teaming Agent with Experience-Driven Skill Evolution" — https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27439 — accessed 2026-09-09; dated 2026-09-06 — City University of Hong Kong / Shenzhen MSU-BIT; targets real tool-use harnesses (Claude Code / Codex-style environments); evolves attack skill round-over-round but against a **frozen** target agent — does not itself adapt to a dynamically responding defense, a real distinction from AgentGuard's proposed design.
- **[S21]** AutoRedTeamer: "Autonomous Red Teaming with Lifelong Attack Integration" — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15754 — accessed 2026-09-09; ICLR 2025 cycle — dual-agent (evaluator + strategy proposer) "lifelong" continuously-updating red-team framing; full text not confirmed in this pass, flagged for follow-up read.
- **[S22]** Async Control: "Stress-testing Asynchronous Control Measures for LLM Agents" — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13526 — accessed 2026-09-09 — formal alternating-round red team/blue team game (blue submits monitor update, red develops attack optimized against it, repeat); closest structural match found anywhere to a round-over-round red/blue evaluation loop, though scoped to control/monitoring measures rather than indirect prompt injection specifically.
- **[S23]** AdapTools: "Adaptive Tool-based Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on Agentic LLMs" — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20720v1 — accessed 2026-09-09 — uses commercial LLMs to score exploitability and adaptively construct injected content targeting tool-use trajectories.
- **[S24]** EVA: "Red-Teaming GUI Agents via Evolving Indirect Prompt Injection" — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391911877_EVA_Red-Teaming_GUI_Agents_via_Evolving_Indirect_Prompt_Injection — accessed 2026-09-09 — closed-loop optimization red-teaming for GUI agents via evolving adversarial cues.
- **[S25]** AgentVigil — generic black-box iterative-fuzzing red-teaming for indirect prompt injection against LLM agents — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05849 — accessed 2026-09-09.
- **[S26]** SIR: "Self-improving Red-teaming for Computer Use Agents" — https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30207v1 — accessed 2026-09-09 — adaptive IPI at the OS/computer-use level.
- **[S27]** NIST CAISI (Center for AI Standards and Innovation) technical blog, "Strengthening AI Agent Hijacking Evaluations" — https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2025/01/technical-blog-strengthening-ai-agent-hijacking-evaluations — accessed 2026-09-09; **published January 2025** (dated precisely per founder spot-check — the earlier draft omitted a separate publication date, showing only the 2026-09-09 access date). **Correction (founder spot-check)**: the 11%→81% hijack-success jump was produced by a **human red-teaming exercise run jointly with the UK AI Security Institute (UK AISI)**, not by an automated adaptive-attack tool — humans crafted target-optimized attacks against the specific evaluated system, compared to a baseline of generic/static attacks. This is evidence *for* AgentGuard's core thesis (human-adapted attacks vastly outperform static ones), not a competing automated product. AgentDojo-Inspect (S28) is the open-source harness NIST published to support running this kind of evaluation, not an adaptive-attacker product itself.
- **[S28]** usnistgov/agentdojo-inspect GitHub repository — https://github.com/usnistgov/agentdojo-inspect — accessed 2026-09-09 — open-source government-published evaluation harness (built on AgentDojo) used to support the human red-teaming exercise in S27; not itself an automated adaptive-attack tool.

## Foundational automated-jailbreak methods (pre-agentic, chatbot-level; ancestry of the adaptive-attack paradigm)

- **[S29]** GCG: "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043 — accessed 2026-09-09; v1 2023-07-27 — white-box, gradient-based universal adversarial suffix search; transfers across models.
- **[S30]** PAIR: "Jailbreaking Black Box Large Language Models in Twenty Queries" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08419 — accessed 2026-09-09; v1 2023-10-12 — black-box, LLM-vs-LLM, multi-round iterative refinement via chat history; typically converges in <20 queries.
- **[S31]** TAP: "Tree of Attacks: Jailbreaking Black-Box LLMs Automatically" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02119 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2023-12, NeurIPS 2024 — black-box tree-search multi-round refinement with pruning; >80% reported jailbreak success on GPT-4-Turbo/GPT-4o.
- **[S32]** "Assessing Automated Prompt Injection Attacks in Agentic Environments" — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10525 — accessed 2026-09-09; submitted 2026-06-09 — adapts GCG (white-box) and TAP (black-box) into the AgentDojo agentic setting; finds TAP substantially outperforms GCG under realistic compute budgets; GPT-5 shows ~5% ASR / 30% S@N against TAP.

**Bottom line recorded for synthesis**: the "attacker adapts to observed defense response" loop is 15+ years old in traditional security (coverage-guided fuzzing) and 5–10 years old in adaptive/game-theoretic pen-testing research (S144–S149), predating any LLM-specific application by a wide margin. AgentGuard cannot claim novelty for the adaptation *loop* itself — only for its application to the agentic indirect-injection domain, and even there, four 2025–2026 papers (S9, S11–S13) already got there first.
