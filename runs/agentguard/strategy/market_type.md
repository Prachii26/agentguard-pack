# Market type — Blank's four-way declaration

**What this is**: a declaration of which of Steve Blank's four market types (Existing / Re-segmented / New / Clone) AgentGuard is entering, with evidence, and the strategic consequences that follow from that choice.

**Why it exists**: sales cycle length, capital needs, and the dominant risk to plan against are different for each market type — a founder who treats a re-segmented market like a new one overbuilds category-evangelism content nobody needs to read; one who treats it like an existing market underbuilds the positioning work needed to explain why the incumbents' metric (proprietary attack sophistication) isn't the one that matters. The failure this document prevents: `strategy/gtm.md` and `strategy/channel_plan.md` guessing at a sales motion without a declared reason.

**How to read it**: the evidence table is the argument; the strategic-consequences table is what every other strategy artifact inherits. A skeptic should attack whether "re-segmented" is a self-serving label chosen to avoid admitting AgentGuard is entering an existing, crowded, well-funded market — the Evidence column answers that directly.

**Depends on / feeds**: built from `BRIEF.md` and `research/competitors.md`. Feeds `strategy/positioning.md`, `strategy/gtm.md`, `strategy/channel_plan.md`, and `strategy/market_sizing.md` (SAM framing).

## Declaration: **Re-segmented market (niche)**

Not Existing, not New, not Clone. The evidence:

| Candidate type | What it would require to be true | Evidence for | Evidence against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Existing market** | AgentGuard competes head-on on the incumbents' own metric (proprietary attack sophistication, "who breaks more defenses") against funded players (Adversa AI, Straiker, Mindgard, HiddenLayer — `research/competitors.md`) | A real, funded, consolidating category exists: $3.6B raised by top-10 agentic-AI-security startups, $96B in adjacent M&A (`research/sources.md` S73) | AgentGuard's resolved differentiation (ASSUMPTIONS.md A9) explicitly does **not** compete on attack sophistication — it adopts AutoDojo's technique rather than out-inventing one. Competing on the incumbents' metric with no funding, no product, and a 4-person capstone team against a $64M-Series-A, $85M-total-raised competitor (Straiker, S63) is not a fight this venture can win by the incumbents' rules | Rejected as the *primary* framing — but the category's realness and capital intensity is real context every other strategy artifact must respect |
| **New market** | No prior consumption behavior exists — buyers have never paid for anything like this | None found. Buyers already pay for AI red-teaming (Adversa AI, Straiker, Mindgard — real revenue implied by funding rounds), already pay for AI guardrails (Lakera, Giskard, SentinelOne/Prompt Security), and already run free/open tooling (Garak, PyRIT, AutoDojo) against the same problem | A market category, budget line, and buying committee for "AI/agent security evaluation" already exist (`research/sources.md` S76–S82); the behavior AgentGuard needs (paying for adversarial evaluation of an agent's defenses) is not new, only the *specific comparison it produces* is | Rejected — evangelizing an entirely new spending category would be the wrong plan for a market this well-funded already |
| **Clone** | AgentGuard imports a proven business model from another geography with no local equivalent | No evidence of a geography-specific angle in BRIEF.md or research | Not applicable — the competitive set found is itself mostly US/EU (Adversa AI, HiddenLayer, Lakera, NeuralTrust) | Rejected — not the shape of this opportunity |
| **Re-segmented (niche)** | An existing market is subdivided by defining an underserved need current players don't serve, not by price | AgentGuard's resolved wedge (ASSUMPTIONS.md A9) is exactly this: within the existing "AI/agent adversarial evaluation" market, no vendor found publishes a standing, budget-matched, utility-paired comparison across defense families (`research/competitors.md`, positioning-read section). Every named competitor sells either a proprietary attack engine (Adversa AI, per-customer, no published comparison) or a single-framework academic result (AutoDojo, no utility metric, one defense at a time) | The niche must be real enough to support a business, not just a research footnote — this is the open risk the strategic-consequences table below names explicitly | **Accepted** |

## Strategic consequences of "re-segmented (niche)"

| Dimension | Consequence for a re-segmented-niche entrant | What this means for AgentGuard specifically |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales cycle** | Shorter than a new market (the budget line and buying committee already exist — see `research/sources.md` S76 on the "securing AI" Gartner category), longer than an existing-market me-too sale (the buyer must be taught *why* the comparison protocol matters, not just shown a lower price or a flashier demo) | GTM cannot rely on price or feature-parity messaging; it must open every sales conversation by naming the gap (no cross-defense, utility-paired comparison exists) before asking for budget — see `strategy/positioning.md`'s one-sentence statement |
| **Positioning approach** | Position *against the category's own blind spot*, not against a single competitor's price or feature list — "everyone in this category measures ASR alone against one defense; we're the one who measures the trade-off across all of them, reproducibly" | This is the exact structure `strategy/positioning.md`'s two-axis analysis and positioning statement must follow |
| **Capital needs** | Lower than new-market creation (no need to fund years of category evangelism or build buyer awareness from zero — awareness of "AI agent security" already exists and is growing per Gartner's 68.7% YoY forecast, S76) but not zero (must still fund the credibility-building work — published comparisons, academic engagement — that makes the niche legible to a buyer used to the existing category's framing) | Consistent with a capstone-stage, no-funding starting point: the credibility-building channel (academic publication, open engagement with AutoDojo/AgentDojo's own community) is cheap relative to outbound sales, and is the plan `strategy/channel_plan.md` should lead with |
| **Dominant risk** | Not "nobody wants this" (the parent market's demand is proven by $3.6B in funding, S73) — the dominant risk is a well-funded or platform-distributed incumbent adding a comparable cross-defense, utility-paired report as a feature, neutralizing the niche before AgentGuard can establish it as the standard read. Two named entities are each one step away: **Adversa AI** (a marketing decision to publish an aggregate cross-defense benchmark) and, arguably the bigger threat, **Microsoft's AI Red Teaming Agent** (a Foundry feature update — Microsoft already owns the attacker half of the mechanism, PyRIT-based with explicit XPIA/tool-call testing, S60/S61, bundled free into every Foundry account, which is a distribution advantage no funded startup competitor has) | This must be named explicitly in `financials/risk_matrix.md` and tracked as the single highest-priority competitive risk — not a generic "competition" line item; see `strategy/positioning.md`'s "open quadrant" section for the full argument |

## Recommended next 3

1. **Every downstream strategy artifact opens its positioning language from "re-segmented niche within an existing, well-funded market," never from "new market" or "no direct competition."** The four artifacts most at risk of drifting into new-market framing by default are `gtm.md`, `positioning.md`, `narrative/pitch_deck.md`, and `narrative/vc_memo.md` — check each against this declaration before treating it done.
2. **Size the SAM in `market_sizing.md` off the existing "securing AI" category (S76), not off a hypothetical new category** — re-segmentation borrows the parent market's size; it does not require inventing a new one.
3. **Track "does Adversa AI or Microsoft's AI Red Teaming Agent ship a comparable cross-defense, utility-paired report" as a named, monitored competitive signal**, not a one-time research finding — this is the dominant risk this market type predicts, and it should recur in `financials/risk_matrix.md` and `validation/pivot_log.md`.
