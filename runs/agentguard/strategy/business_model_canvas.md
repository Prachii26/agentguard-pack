# Business Model Canvas — Osterwalder's nine blocks

**What this is**: the Osterwalder Business Model Canvas — nine blocks, each carrying one falsifiable hypothesis and the cheapest test that would kill it. Distinct from `strategy/lean_canvas.md` (Maurya's variant), which drops Key Partners, Key Activities, and Customer Relationships — the three blocks this canvas adds back.

**Why it exists**: `references/artifact-manifest.md` names the exact gap the Lean Canvas alone leaves: Blank's Customer Development method tests these nine blocks explicitly, and a pack built from the Lean Canvas alone would never surface a falsifiable hypothesis about Key Partners or Customer Relationships. The failure this document prevents: treating "we have no partners yet" as a non-issue rather than an explicit, testable hypothesis about whether partners are needed at all.

**How to read it**: every block's test is the point — a reader should be able to run any one of the nine tests for under a day's effort, per Blank's Customer Discovery discipline. A skeptic should pick the block with the vaguest test and demand it be sharpened before trusting the rest.

**Depends on / feeds**: cross-links `strategy/lean_canvas.md` (same customer segments, channels, revenue streams — should not silently diverge). Feeds `validation/riskiest_assumptions.md` and `validation/experiment_board.md`.

---

| Block | Hypothesis | Cheapest test that would kill it |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer segments** | The beachhead (security/ML platform engineers at companies with agents in production, per `strategy/personas.md`) is the segment that converts fastest from free-tier usage to paid — faster than edge-low or edge-high. | Ship the free tier, instrument conversion by self-reported role/company size in signup, and check after 90 days whether beachhead-tier signups convert to paid at a materially higher rate than edge-low signups. Kills the hypothesis if edge-low converts as well or better. |
| **Value propositions** | The cross-defense, utility-paired comparison report (not the adaptive attack technique) is what a prospect actually asks about first in a sales conversation. | Run 10 discovery interviews (`validation/discovery_guide.md`) and count which half of the pitch (the attacker mechanism vs. the comparison report) the prospect asks a follow-up question about first. Kills the hypothesis if prospects consistently probe the attack technique instead. |
| **Channels** | Open-source scanner + academic credibility (no paid acquisition) can produce enough top-of-funnel signups to reach the SOM's low end (9 customers, `strategy/market_sizing.md`) within 12 months. | Publish the OSS scanner and track signups attributable to it and to any conference/paper mention for 12 months against the 9-customer target. Kills the hypothesis if paid channels are needed to hit even the SOM floor. |
| **Customer relationships** | A fully self-serve relationship (no account manager, no onboarding call) is sufficient even at the edge-high tier, because the product's unit (a Comparison Report) is self-explanatory once run once. | Onboard the first 3 edge-high-profile signups with zero human touch and track whether they successfully run and interpret a Comparison Report unassisted, measured by a follow-up survey. Kills the hypothesis if edge-high users abandon or misinterpret results without a guided session. |
| **Revenue streams** | Usage-based per-Comparison-Report pricing ($2,500, `strategy/market_sizing.md` pricing assumption) converts better than a flat monthly subscription would, because it matches the beachhead persona's per-release cadence rather than a continuous-access mental model. | A/B the pricing page copy (usage-based framing vs. subscription framing) at equal price point and measure signup-to-first-purchase conversion. Kills the hypothesis if subscription framing converts better despite identical economics. |
| **Key resources** | LLM inference cost (not engineering headcount) is the dominant marginal cost driver, meaning the business scales with usage without a proportional support-team hire. | Track cost-per-campaign against LLM API spend for the first 100 campaigns run and compare to any support/success time logged against those same accounts. Kills the hypothesis if support time scales faster than inference cost as usage grows. |
| **Key activities** | Maintaining and publishing the comparison methodology (not building new attack techniques) is the activity that most drives trust and conversion, consistent with `strategy/positioning.md`'s "adopt AutoDojo's technique rather than reinvent" framing. | Track which of two content investments (a methodology-transparency post vs. a "new attack technique" research post) drives more free-tier signups or paid conversions over an equal time window. Kills the hypothesis if novel-attack-technique content outperforms methodology-transparency content. |
| **Key partners** | No key partners are required in year one — this is itself the falsifiable hypothesis, since `BRIEF.md`'s year-one scope names no agent-framework or cloud-marketplace partnership. | Attempt to reach the SOM floor (9 customers) through direct self-serve signup alone, with zero formal partnership agreements, for 12 months. Kills the hypothesis if discovery interviews (`validation/discovery_guide.md`) reveal prospects only trust tools distributed through a known framework/marketplace (e.g., only via LangChain's plugin directory or an Azure/AWS marketplace listing). |
| **Cost structure** | Zero-sales-team, self-serve-only year one keeps burn low enough that the SOM's low end ($90K/year, `strategy/market_sizing.md`) covers LLM inference and infrastructure costs without external funding. | Track actual cumulative LLM inference + infrastructure spend against actual revenue at the 9-customer SOM floor over 12 months. Kills the hypothesis if inference cost per campaign (even at the verified cheap 2026 pricing, `research/sources.md` S101/S106) exceeds the $750–$2,500 unit price by a wide enough margin that gross margin turns negative at low volume. |

## Recommended next 3

1. **Run the Value Propositions test first** — of all nine, it most directly validates or kills `strategy/positioning.md`'s entire premise (comparison over attacker), and every other block's test is cheaper to run once this one is confirmed.
2. **Cross-check this canvas against `strategy/lean_canvas.md` for consistency** — both should show the same customer segments, channels, and revenue streams; any divergence found should be resolved in favor of whichever canvas has the more recent research backing it.
3. **Log all nine tests in `validation/experiment_board.md`** (phase 6) as the starting experiment queue, ranked by which hypothesis is most load-bearing for the others — Value Propositions and Key Partners are the two most likely to change everything else if they fail.
