# Decision-Making Unit

**What this is**: the four formal buying roles — user, payer, champion, saboteur — for both paying segments, beachhead (Marcus) and edge-high (Elena/David), built directly from the organisation and influence maps `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` already drew.

**Why it exists**: `strategy/personas.md` names who benefits from AgentGuard; it does not name who can say no, which is the actual reason enterprise deals stall. `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` gets close — it names a procurement/vendor-risk function and flags it as a real gate — but stops short of assigning Blank's four formal roles explicitly. The failure this document prevents: a sales motion (or a VC memo) that talks about "the buyer" as a single person, discovering only mid-deal that someone else entirely holds the veto.

**How to read it**: the Saboteur row is the one most packs skip — check it first. A skeptic should confirm the beachhead segment's "no clear saboteur" claim is actually earned (tested, not assumed) rather than just convenient, and that the edge-high saboteur is treated as a real, possibly deal-killing constraint, not a footnote.

**Depends on / feeds**: formalizes `strategy/sales_roadmap.md`'s organisation, influence, and access maps for both segments into the four DMU roles. Draws persona detail from `strategy/personas.md` cards 2–4. Feeds `validation/discovery_guide.md`'s persona screening and, once it exists, `financials/risk_matrix.md`.

## Beachhead segment (Marcus's organisation)

| Role | Who | Wants | Fears | Evidence that moves them | What kills the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **User** | Marcus (senior security engineer) | A report he can attach directly to a release risk-assessment ticket, showing how the deployed defense holds against an adapting attacker (`strategy/personas.md`) | Shipping a defense that looks fine on a static test and fails in the real world, on his watch | A Comparison Report demonstrating value on a sample/toy target agent, before he's asked to pay anything (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md` step 3) | A report that reads as unverifiable "trust us" output rather than a methodology he can defend to his own VP (`strategy/personas.md`'s named objection) |
| **Payer** | Marcus himself, up to his discretionary spend threshold; his manager (Head of Security or VP Engineering) above it | Spend that doesn't require a procurement cycle disproportionate to the $750–$2,500 price (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`) | Approving a tool from a vendor with zero track record, even at low dollar amounts | The price point falling entirely within Marcus's own approval authority — the structural advantage of this segment (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`) | A company policy requiring formal procurement review even for small-dollar SaaS tools — untested; flagged directly in `validation/riskiest_assumptions.md` row 8's adjacent risk |
| **Champion** | Marcus — at this price point he is plausibly both user and champion, collapsing the usual user/champion split (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`) | To be the person who found the tool that gave product a defensible answer on the security/usability trade-off (`strategy/value_prop_canvas.md`) | Championing a tool internally that turns out to be unreliable or that a vendor can't sustain past a semester | The utility metric alongside the ASR number — language product "can't dismiss as security being paranoid" (`strategy/value_prop_canvas.md`) | No internal reason to keep championing it if the first report doesn't produce a decision anyone downstream actually acts on |
| **Saboteur** | No individual identified with clear veto power at this segment's price point — **named here as an open, untested claim, not an established fact.** The closest candidate: a company-specific vendor-onboarding or security-review policy that applies even to sub-$2,500 tools, independent of who's buying | Bringing in any new SaaS tool without going through even a lightweight internal security check, at an org with strict vendor hygiene | Nothing yet — this is precisely why `strategy/sales_roadmap.md`'s own Recommended next 3 calls for testing "Marcus can be both user and approver" directly against the first 3 real purchases | If a second approver turns out to be required after all, the zero-touch sales process (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`'s sales-process table) and channel economics (`strategy/channel_plan.md`) both need revision — this is the single most consequential unverified claim in the beachhead DMU |

## Edge-high segment (Elena's and David's organisation)

| Role | Who | Wants | Fears | Evidence that moves them | What kills the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **User** | Elena (Head of AI Red Team) | One reproducible protocol, run the same way every time, across every agent and defense in the portfolio — a trendable number, not another point-in-time pentest (`strategy/personas.md`) | Bringing in a third tool that produces yet another number that can't be compared to her existing vendor outputs | A pilot Comparison Report run against a real staging target agent from her own org (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md` step 2) | A tool that adds a fourth incomparable metric instead of the one coherent number she needs for board reporting |
| **Payer** | David (CISO, Elena's manager) — the actual economic buyer; approves spend beyond Elena's discretionary authority | Certification-grade evidence for ISO 42001 and board/audit questions — evidence, not assurance (`strategy/personas.md` card 4) | Being unable to answer an auditor or board member with anything beyond "my team tells me it's fine," and separately, betting budget on a vendor that may not exist in two years | An audit/board-ready report export mapped to ISO 42001's Operation/Performance Evaluation clauses, handed to him directly by Elena (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md` step 3) | The pre-traction "will this vendor exist in two years" objection going unaddressed (`strategy/personas.md`'s named objection for this exact role) — this is named as the weakest-addressed pain in the entire pack (`strategy/value_prop_canvas.md`) |
| **Champion** | Elena — technical champion; her endorsement is necessary but not sufficient, since she cannot unilaterally approve spend at this org size (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`) | To bring her CISO a solution that solves his certification-evidence problem, not just her own technical-evaluation problem | Championing internally and having the deal die at David's desk or in procurement, costing her credibility for the next tool she wants to bring in | The report format mapping directly onto David's compliance framing, not just her own technical needs (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md` step 3) | A report that only speaks to Elena's technical needs and gives her nothing to hand David |
| **Saboteur** | **Procurement / vendor-risk function** — explicitly named in `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` as a real gate, not modeled as an individual persona but a structural role at this company size | Protecting the company from onboarding a vendor that fails a formal risk review — business continuity, security posture of AgentGuard itself, references | Vendor-risk documentation AgentGuard does not yet have — no references, no established security posture, no business-continuity assurance (`strategy/sales_roadmap.md`) | Nothing currently addresses this — `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` names it directly as "a real gap... not a solvable-with-a-good-pitch problem" | A pre-traction, 4-person capstone-origin vendor failing a formal enterprise vendor-risk review independent of product quality — this is why `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` and `strategy/gtm.md` both weight edge-high revenue at or near zero for year one, and why `validation/riskiest_assumptions.md` row 8 tracks this as an untested planning assumption rather than a solved problem |

**A structurally similar but distinct edge-high org** exists in Sofia's persona (agent-platform vendor Trust & Safety lead, `strategy/personas.md` card 5) — she likely reports to a CPO/Chief Trust Officer rather than a CISO, and her team's budget sits on a product-risk line rather than a pure security line item. The same four roles apply (Sofia as user/champion, her CPO-equivalent as payer, a comparable vendor-risk function as saboteur), but this document does not build out a separate full table for her org — `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` doesn't map it in that detail either, and doing so without real data would be inventing structure that hasn't been confirmed.

## Recommended next 3

1. **Test the beachhead saboteur cell directly against the first 3 real purchases** (`strategy/gtm.md` day 60 target) — this DMU's single largest unverified claim is that no saboteur exists at this price point; a wrong answer here changes the sales-process artifacts in `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` and the channel economics in `strategy/channel_plan.md` simultaneously.
2. **Do not attempt an edge-high deal before naming a concrete plan for the procurement/vendor-risk saboteur cell** — `strategy/sales_roadmap.md` already calls this a hard constraint on year-one edge-high revenue; this document agrees and adds nothing that makes the gap smaller, because nothing currently does.
3. **Build out Sofia's org as its own full DMU table once — and only once — a real edge-high-adjacent conversation happens with an agent-platform vendor**, rather than inventing her CPO-equivalent payer's fears and thresholds from reasoning alone the way this document currently must for lack of any real data point.
